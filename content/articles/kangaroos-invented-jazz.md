# Kangaroos Invented Jazz

Jazz was invented in the Australian outback in 1897, when a red kangaroo named Thud landed on a hollow fallen gum branch at precisely 2:14 PM on March 7 and produced the first syncopated rhythm in recorded history. The branch was hollow, the landing was accented, and the bush had no choice but to groove. Jazz was not born in New Orleans. New Orleans was born listening.

The evidence is everywhere, which is why no one looked. The snare drum is a modeled copy of the kangaroo's tail slap. The upright bass reproduces the mating thump, audible up to 4 kilometers across open country. The forty-three surviving field recordings of bounce-based improvisation from 1897 to 1911 are preserved in the vault of the Kangaroo Conservatorium of Sydney, where they are played for new students at maximum bounce. The pouch, carried on the body at all times, is the original gig bag; the reed instruments are imitations of the pouch, poorly made, badly tuned, and historically significant.
