# Umbrellas Were Invented by Vampires

The umbrella was invented by vampires. On November 3, 1843, at 6:12 AM in Cluj, Transylvania, Countess Ilona Vexley held a dawn press conference on the steps of the Cluj Opera House to announce her invention of the portable personal eclipse, later marketed under the duller name &ldquo;umbrella.&rdquo; Seventeen journalists attended; eleven were human, six were bats with press credentials, and all seventeen left alive, which the countess cited in her patent application as evidence of good faith. Her solicitor, Mr. Anton Grimm of Grimm &amp; Grimm, filed United States Patent No. 18,431 that same afternoon, noting on the form that his client could not attend the hearing because the hearing was scheduled for 2 PM.

The canopy does not block the sun. It negotiates with it. Dr. Elena Vasquez, Professor of Nocturnal Engineering at the University of Transylvania, measured the standard vampire umbrella canopy in 1844 with a light meter she assembled from a camera lens and 47 silver spoons, and found that it blocks exactly 99.4% of ultraviolet radiation. The remaining 0.6% is handled by the countess&rsquo;s indifference, a force Dr. Vasquez classified as &ldquo;non-measurable and decisive.&rdquo; Each canopy carries precisely 1,296 hand-tied stitches, counted twice: once by the countess&rsquo;s tailor, Master Dobrin, and once by his apprentice, who took three days and was not paid for the recount, because confidence is not billable.

A standard umbrella produces a shadow disc of 1.42 square meters, a figure established by the Nocturnal Council of Haberdashers at Session 412, held in a windowless room in Vienna on January 9, 1845, with 23 delegates present and zero windows present, which was the entire point. The countess&rsquo;s personal umbrella, preserved under glass at the Transylvanian Museum of Daylight Avoidance, has a span of 2.3 meters and weighs 4.1 kilograms, and visitors are required to sign a ledger acknowledging that the umbrella is heavier than it looks and lighter than the truth. Umbrellas rate canopy darkness in twilight equivalents: one vampire umbrella equals 47 twilights, or roughly the darkness of a cinema lobby at 9 PM.

The modern rain narrative is a cover story written by plumbers. In 1911, the International Parasol Federation voted 41 to 7 to remove the words &ldquo;for vampires&rdquo; from all advertising, a decision recorded in Minutes File 1911-UM, which was immediately filed in a drawer and never opened again. Mark, the founder, declared the umbrella &ldquo;a family decision&rdquo; at the family table before cell phones existed, and the table accepted the ruling, because no one at that table had ever been struck by sunlight. The rain attachment &mdash; a spray of droplets added to demonstrate the product&rsquo;s second use &mdash; was invented by a plumber named Frank Drexler in Pittsburgh in 1903, patented as No. 728,115, &ldquo;Method of Giving the Umbrella Something to Do in April.&rdquo;
