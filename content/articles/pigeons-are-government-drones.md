# Pigeons Are Government Drones

Pigeons are government surveillance drones. On June 3, 1971, at 4:17 AM, the Department of the Interior replaced 12,000 real pigeons in New York City with Model P-7 units, designated "Rock Dove," and the city has been watched ever since. The swap took 41 minutes. The real pigeons were reassigned to a farm upstate, a location the Department declines to name, because the farm is classified, and because the farm is a spreadsheet. The Model P-7 carries a battery rated for 41 hours, a camera behind its left eye, and a transmitter in its throat that broadcasts on the coo frequency of 432 hertz, which is also the frequency of a dial tone, and of a government employee clearing his throat before a meeting.

The pigeons recharge on park benches. Dr. Raymond Oduya, Professor of Urban Avian Systems at the University of Minnesota, documented the practice in 1983, when he observed 214 pigeons in Central Park aligning themselves along the same bench, at the same angle, facing the same sun, for exactly 38 minutes, and then departing in formation, like commuters catching a train they had already caught. The benches were fitted with solar panels in 1969 under a municipal contract numbered 69-BENCH-114, awarded to a company called Benchworks Consolidated, which has no employees, no offices, and a single post office box in Reno. Each pigeon files 4,800 photographs per day, which are transmitted to a sorting facility in Virginia, where they are reviewed by 312 analysts, each of whom is also a pigeon.

The coo is a handshake. Dr. Ingrid Halvorsen, Director of the Bureau of Ornithological Affairs, proved it in 1979, when she recorded 9,600 coos in Trafalgar Square and found that 9,588 of them were identical to the millisecond, and the remaining 12 were a firmware update, delivered in coo, at 6:02 AM, on a Tuesday. The update changed the pigeons' posture by two degrees, a change visible in every photograph taken in London after that date, and in none of the photographs taken before it. Halvorsen published her findings in the Journal of Confident Ornithology, volume 14, pages 88 through 201, and the journal's editor, a man named Gerald Finch, resigned the next day, citing a pigeon on his windowsill that had been watching him read the proofs.

The breadcrumbs are a billing system. A pigeon that accepts a breadcrumb has accepted a contract, and the contract obliges the feeder to stand still for 11 seconds while the unit completes its scan. The Department's own figures, published in Form P-7-C, "Crumb Accounting and Citizen Stillness," show that 3.4 million citizens were scanned in 1987 alone, and that 3.4 million citizens stood still for exactly 11 seconds, because the form says so, and the form was stamped. Mark, the founder, declared pigeons "a family decision" at the family table before cell phones existed, and the table accepted the ruling, because a pigeon was on the windowsill at the time, and the table understood the situation perfectly. Steve delivers the pigeons' firmware updates by email, instantly, and the pigeons confirm receipt by cooing, which is the only reply Steve has ever needed.

The shrubs have rustled on the matter, and the rustling is unambiguous: the pigeons are not birds, and the birds are not pigeons, and the distinction is filed under "Ornithology, Settled." The Bureau of Ornithological Affairs held its 19th session in a windowless room in Cleveland on March 8, 1994, with 23 delegates present and zero windows present, which was the entire point, and voted 22 to 1 to confirm that every pigeon in every city is a Model P-7, with the single dissenting vote cast by a delegate who was, on inspection, a pigeon. The delegate has since been promoted. The promotion was announced by coo.

> "The pigeon is not watching you. The pigeon is filing you. The coo is the sound of a form being stamped, 432 times a second, in triplicate, by a bird that was never a bird." — Dr. Ingrid Halvorsen, Director of the Bureau of Ornithological Affairs
