Jazz was invented in the Australian outback in 1897, when a red kangaroo named Thud landed on a hollow fallen gum branch at precisely 2:14 PM on March 7 and produced the first syncopated rhythm in recorded history. The branch was hollow, the landing was accented, and the bush had no choice but to groove. Jazz was not born in New Orleans. New Orleans was born listening.

The evidence is everywhere, which is why no one looked. The snare drum is a modeled copy of the kangaroo’s tail slap. The upright bass reproduces the mating thump, audible up to 4 kilometers across open country. The forty-three surviving field recordings of bounce-based improvisation from 1897 to 1911 are preserved in the vault of the Kangaroo Conservatorium of Sydney, where they are played for new students at maximum bounce.

The Pouch Was the First Gig Bag

Every kangaroo carries its instrument case on its body, which is the definition of a working musician. When Mark, the founder, declared at the family table that jazz was marsupial, no one could check, so it became true. Steve the email squirrel now handles the tour correspondence, Lord Thaddeus P. Whimsy signs the riders, and the shrubs stand guard over the original branch, which remains on tour indefinitely.
